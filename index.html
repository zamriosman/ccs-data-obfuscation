Two ideas to remember

Obfuscation vs masking

Obfuscation is the policy: which technique applies to which data. Masking is one technique inside it.

Three questions decide the technique

  1. Must it be reversible? Encryption, tokenization and FPE: yes. Masking and shuffling: usually no.
  2. Must the format stay the same? Masking, tokenization and FPE: yes. Encryption: no.
  3. Will it feed analytics? Shuffling keeps distributions. Masking and tokenization can break joins. Encryption breaks most analytics.

How it works

  1. Pick the sensitive field.
  2. Apply a rule: hide part of it, or swap it for a realistic fake.
  3. Static: save a masked copy. Dynamic: mask at query time for unauthorised users.

How it works

  1. An algorithm plus a key turns plaintext into unreadable ciphertext.
  2. Symmetric: one shared key, fast, harder to manage at scale.
  3. Asymmetric: public key encrypts, private key decrypts. Slower, easier to manage securely.

How it works

  1. Split the digits into two halves.
  2. Run several key-driven rounds that mix one half into the other, staying inside 0–9.
  3. Output keeps the same length and pattern, so strict validators still accept it. The key reverses it.
